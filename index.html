How many generations lie between us and the past?

Two people looking back to the same year may count a different number of mothers in their ancestral lines. What changes when those lines meet at a shared ancestor?

01 / The question

A family tree is not a collection of independent paths.

A maternal generation is a step from a person to their mother. Since maternal ages vary, the number of steps to reach a historical date also varies. Shared ancestors introduce an additional dependence: different people can inherit the same part of an ancestral path.

01 · TIME

Count generations, not years

The same calendar interval can contain different numbers of maternal generations.

02 · ANCESTRY

Allow lineages to meet

Two apparently distinct paths may join at one historical mother, after which they share ancestry.

03 · COHORTS

Look beyond one person

The dependence between lineages matters when studying differences across an entire cohort.

02 / One result

Shared ancestry can change predicted dispersion.

In a fully specified homogeneous mathematical model, accounting for coalescence changes the asymptotic coefficient for the expected mean pairwise difference in maternal generation counts across a cohort.

For the paper's illustrative parameters, the coefficient is about 13% lower with shared ancestry than in the independent-renewal comparison.
